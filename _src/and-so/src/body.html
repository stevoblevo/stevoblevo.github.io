Stories

    Liner notes

    The jam is built like a Jamey Aebersold Play-A-Long: a rhythm section and a chord chart, with the melody left out for you. Aebersold, born July 21, 1939, started the series in 1967; it runs to more than 130 volumes, and the piano and bass sit in opposite channels so you can turn either one off. He was named an NEA Jazz Master in 2014. Here the bass walks on the right and the voices on the terrain comp.

    The changes are in tunings a piano can't play. Research summarized on Wikipedia puts the blue third anywhere from 7/6 (267¢) to about 350¢, with Cutting's analysis of early blues recordings centering it at 6/5 (316¢), often slurred toward 5/4 (386¢), which Temperley and colleagues call a neutral third; the blue fifth most often at 7/5 (583¢); the blue seventh at 7/4 (969¢, after Kubik and Curry) or, more commonly in Cutting's data, 9/5 (1018¢). Kubik and Curry also suggest 11/8 (551¢). Those are the blue keys on the pad.

    Ethan Hein puts the theory in one line: “blues tonality comes from the overtone series of I and IV.” The twelve-bar jam builds every chord as 4:5:6:7, a slice of one overtone series, on 1/1, 4/3 and 3/2. The turnaround uses 31 equal steps, where 25 steps is 967.7¢, 1.1¢ under the barbershop seventh.

    1955 inside AND SO (get a voice up to eighty-eight) is boogie-woogie in just intonation. Clarence “Pinetop” Smith recorded “Pinetop's Boogie Woogie” on December 29, 1928, in Chicago; Vocalion released it on March 1, 1929, the first known record with “boogie” in the title. Chuck Berry's “Johnny B. Goode” (Chess, March 31, 1958) opens with a riff borrowed from the single-note solo Carl Hogan played on Louis Jordan's “Ain't That Just Like a Woman” (1946), and Marty McFly plays it at the dance in Back to the Future (1985). These are citations: none of those recordings, melodies or lyrics is reproduced here.

    Microtonal pop has precedent: King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard's Flying Microtonal Banana (February 24, 2017) is in quarter tones, 24 equal steps, played on guitars refretted for it; the idea began with the Turkish bağlama.
